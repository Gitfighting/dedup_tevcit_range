\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

With the prevalence of public-cloud object storage, geo-distributed clients pay for bytes stored over time and bytes moved across regions.
This paper reviews object-granularity TevictRanges and analyzes its limitation: when objects share content, SkyStore and even its object-level Optimal store and move the same chunks under independent object leases.
Thus, we propose ChunkTevict, which keeps the TevictRanges histogram, $T_{\mathrm{evict}}$ estimator, and {TTL} rule, and moves placement, eviction, transfer, and {GB}$\cdot$h accounting to unique chunks per region.
Finally, we implement an offline chunk index and a trace replay, and evaluate the five {IBM} traces of SkyStore Fig.~5 bound to versioned public content.
The results show that two-region SkyStore costs $2.18$--$11.80\times$ ChunkTevict and object-level Optimal $2.27$--$11.73\times$, with $88$--$97\%$ of the gap in storage.
In three regions, SkyStore averages $6.40\times$ ChunkTevict with a fixed base and $5.00\times$ under free placement, so the gap is not an artifact of pinning the origin copy.
How much of this carries over to production depends on how much content real workloads share and on request charges for chunk-level access; measuring both on payload-bearing traces is our next step.
